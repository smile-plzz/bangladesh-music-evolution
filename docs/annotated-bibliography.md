# Annotated Bibliography

APA 7th-edition entries for the project's scholarly sources, with an annotation
stating what each contributes and how it is used. This standardises the informal
citations in `docs/literature-review.md`, which remains the working document
where new sources are triaged before being formalised here.

**Verification status.** Every entry below was checked against a bibliographic
record during the literature passes recorded in the review. Two known issues are
carried forward and marked rather than silently resolved: the Pervez (2012)
venue-name discrepancy, and the Mitra (2014) thesis, whose repository record did
not resolve. Direct page fetching was blocked by the network policy in the
session that produced this document, so no entry was re-verified against full
text here; entries reflect the state of the review at its last full pass.

## Bangladeshi rock, metal and popular music

**Autul, M. R., et al. (2024). Rocking across borders: An analysis of the
musical differences between Bangladesh and West-Bengal rock songs using Spotify
audio features. *International Journal of Computer and Digital Systems*.**

The closest methodological precedent for this project's quantitative ambitions:
extracts Spotify audio features from Bangladeshi and West Bengal rock songs and
applies machine-learning classification, reporting greater popularity for the
Bangladeshi sample. Used here as the model for the audio-feature work this
project could not perform, and as the benchmark any future sound-evolution
analysis should be measured against. *Full text located but not machine-readable
in the project environment; methodology section not yet extracted.*

**Hasan, M. (2015, December 4). Rock 'n' roll, social change and democratisation
in Bangladesh. *South Asia @ LSE*.**

A blog essay, not peer-reviewed — flagged as such after an earlier pass in this
project mis-cited it as a journal article. Argues Bangladeshi rock fostered
democratic consciousness and secular attitudes outside state-controlled
institutions. Used as a commentary source for the socio-political dimension,
with its claims triangulated before use rather than cited as evidence.

**Hasan, M., & Kundu, P. (2021). Conformers and rebels: Competing patterns among
Bangladeshi rap musicians. In *Masks of authoritarianism: Hegemony, power and
public life in Bangladesh* (pp. 173–183).**

Interviews with four leading Bangladeshi hip-hop artists plus song analysis.
Finds a generational split: younger, unmarried artists explicit about social
injustice and democratic deficit; older, employed artists toning political
content down. The key source for the hip-hop strand's political dimension, and
the source against which this project's own localisation-marker counts for that
strand were found to be a documentation artefact (see
`analysis/case-studies/hip-hop-lineage.md`).

**Hasan, M., & Kundu, P. (2022). Hip-hop music activism: A new phenomenon in
Bangladeshi popular culture. In *The emergence of Bangladesh: Interdisciplinary
perspectives* (pp. 405–417). Palgrave Macmillan.**

Frames Bangladeshi rap as the latest chapter in a national protest-music
tradition, influenced by American political hip-hop but articulating
Bangladesh-specific criticism. Supports the project's argument that hip-hop
localises on a vernacular-political register rather than a literary one.

**Mitra, U. (2008). Image of urban youth in the lyrics of Bangla bands:
Contradictions and ambivalences. *West Bengal Sociological Review, 1*, 112–113.**

Accessible companion piece to the author's thesis below; used as the citable
proxy while the thesis full text remains unretrieved.

**Mitra, U. (2014). *Exploring youth: A sociological study of Bangla bands*
[Doctoral dissertation, Jadavpur University]. Shodhganga.**

Sociological study of Bangla bands and youth culture, West Bengal-focused and
therefore comparative rather than directly evidential for Bangladesh.
*Repository record did not resolve at time of access; retrieval outstanding.*

**Mridha, M. A. H., & Begum, M. (2023). Continuum of folk to pop music in
Bangladesh: A simple analysis. *Issues in Social Science, 11*(2).
https://doi.org/10.5296/iss.v11i2.21244**

Traces Bengali music from Baul, Bhatiali and Bhawaiya through Rabindra Sangeet
and Nazrul Geeti to modern and band music, framing band music as a recent
development inside a much longer folk continuum. This project's finding that
folk motifs are documented in 20 of 63 catalogued acts across every strand is
the quantitative counterpart to that claim.

**Mukherjee, K. (2017). Bangla rock: Exploring the counterculture and dissidence
in post-colonial Bengali popular music. *International Journal of Pedagogy,
Innovation and New Technologies, 4*(2), 35–47.**

West Bengal-focused, but supplies the theoretical framing for vernacular rock as
countercultural expression of youth dissent. Comparative use only.

**Pervez, A. (2012). Music and identity: A frame analysis of contemporary
popular Bangladeshi music. *Journal of Bangladesh Studies, 137*, 42–55.**

Applies Sinding-Larsen's externalisation of knowledge, frame analysis and Hall's
theory of representation to Bangladeshi music, identifying folk blended with
Western instrumentation as the most popular form and reading music as text for
identity formation. Bridges the Local Adaptation and Listener Preference
dimensions of the BMEM. *Venue name unreconciled: cited elsewhere as* Journal of
Social Studies.

**Quader, S. B. (2016). Forms of capital in the Dhaka metal scene. *Metal Music
Studies, 2*(1), 5–24. https://doi.org/10.1386/mms.2.1.5_1**

Applies Bourdieu's forms of capital — economic, cultural, social, subcultural —
to the Dhaka metal scene, based on 14 participant interviews plus gig
observation and text analysis. Supplies the Bourdieusian grounding that would
otherwise remain an abstract citation in the theoretical section.

**Quader, S. B., & Redden, G. (2015). Approaching the underground: The
production of alternatives in the Bangladeshi metal scene. *Cultural Studies,
29*(3), 401–424. https://doi.org/10.1080/09502386.2014.937945**

The anchor source for the metal strand. Ethnographic study of Dhaka's
underground metal scene arguing that urban youths channelled frustration with
national conditions into building an alternative cultural space; uses "scene"
rather than "subculture" as the analytic frame. Directly supports this
project's reading of Warfaze's cover-to-original pivot and the scene's
cover-band origins. *Note: the project's earlier "2014" citation was the
online-first date; the print citation is 2015.*

**Quader, S. B. (n.d.). *Headbanging in Dhaka: An exploration of the
Bangladeshi alternative music scene* [Doctoral dissertation, University of
Sydney].**

Source thesis behind both Quader publications above.

**Yoon, J.-S. (2019). Popular music of Bangladesh: Cultural hybridity and
identity through the fusion band BANGLA. *South Asia Studies, 25*(2), 59–116.**

Korean-language study tracing Bangladeshi popular music by genre and analysing
the band BANGLA's reworking of Fakir Lalon Shah's Baul repertoire with Western
instrumentation as "glocal cultural content." Directly relevant to the folk
fusion strand and to this project's proposed internal-adaptation pathway.
*Citation reconciled against the publisher record; an earlier "Journal of South
Asian Studies" attribution was a translation of the Korean journal title, not a
separate publication.*

## Theoretical sources

Cited as lenses rather than as evidence about Bangladesh. Each is listed with
the specific use this project makes of it, so that a lens is not carried simply
because it is conventional.

**Bourdieu, P.** — cultural, social and subcultural capital. *Use:* taste and
distinction in scene formation, instantiated empirically by Quader (2016) rather
than applied directly.

**Rogers, E. M.** — diffusion of innovations. *Use:* the format-adaptation
mechanism in BMEF (cassette → CD → streaming → single-first release).

**Hall, S.** — representation and identity. *Use:* via Pervez (2012), for how
musical texts carry cultural identity.

**Tajfel, H., & Turner, J. C.** — social identity theory. *Use:* genre-based
fan community formation. **Currently unsupported by this project's data** — no
listener-side evidence exists here, so the lens is declared and not applied.

**Uses and gratifications theory** — *Use:* would frame listener motivation.
**Also unsupported by this project's data**; retained in the proposal, not
carried into the findings.

## Secondary and journalistic sources

Essential for timeline construction and for the concert database, and not
peer-reviewed. Used with triangulation where possible.

- *The Daily Star* — band histories, Joy Bangla Concert coverage 2017–2024, artist profiles.
- *The Business Standard* — Warfaze anniversary and Ekushey Padak coverage, Joy Bangla Concert, F Minor, Arekta Rock Band.
- *Dhaka Tribune* (including `archive.dhakatribune.com`) — Joy Bangla Concert lineups 2017–2019, AvoidRafa interview.
- *New Age*, *United News of Bangladesh*, *The Independent*, *Daily Sun*, *The Financial Express* — event coverage and artist profiles.
- Wikipedia band and festival pages — starting points, treated as leads to verify rather than as sources.
- ASEF / culture360, *Contemporary music in Bangladesh* (2017).
- Haque, F., & Aman, M. *Banglar Rock Metal* — chronological profiles of roughly 100 bands. **Not yet consulted; the highest-value uncollected source for closing the artist catalogue toward its 300 target.**

## Gaps in the literature this project addresses

1. No prior multi-genre, data-driven mapping of the Bangladeshi ecosystem —
   the existing work is single-scene ethnography (metal) or historical survey.
2. No prior work linking sonic and repertoire change to concert co-appearance
   structure.
3. Almost no computational work on Bangladeshi music; Autul et al. (2024) is the
   sole precedent and is comparative rather than ecosystem-level.
4. Until the 2021–2022 Hasan & Kundu chapters, no academic literature on the
   hip-hop strand at all — and still none on the pop strand, which remains the
   least-theorised part of the ecosystem.
