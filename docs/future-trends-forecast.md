# Future Trends Forecast

Forecasts extrapolated from the catalogue, each stated with the evidence it
rests on and the conditions that would falsify it. Where the project has no
evidence, the entry says so rather than filling the gap with plausible
generalities about streaming.

**Base:** 63 artists, 23 events, 1972–2026. Recency and Dhaka bias apply
throughout. Forecasts are graded by how much of their support is
in this dataset:

- **Grounded** — supported by a measured trend in the catalogue.
- **Indicated** — supported by two or three data points running one direction.
- **Speculative** — consistent with the data but not supported by it.

---

## 1. The single release becomes the default unit · **Grounded**

Singles are absent from the catalogue before the 2010s and are 4 of 12 dated
2020s releases. Arekta Rock Band's trajectory — audience built on singles,
album released at its own headline show rather than through a label campaign —
is the pattern in miniature.

*Expect:* new acts to accumulate singles for years before an album, and album
releases to become live events rather than distribution events.
*Falsified if:* studio-album share holds above ~70% of releases through 2030.

## 2. The festival circuit decentralises out of Dhaka · **Indicated**

19 of 23 catalogued events are in Dhaka. Three data points run the other way:
the 2016 RockNation Sylhet tour date, Chattogram's Bay of Bengal opening the
2019 Joy Bangla Concert, and the 2024 Joy Bangla Concert relocating to
Chattogram's MA Aziz Stadium — the first edition outside the capital.

*Expect:* more regional editions of national festivals, and more regional acts
reaching national bills.
*Falsified if:* subsequent editions return to Dhaka exclusively.
*Caveat:* three events is a direction, not a trend.

## 3. Cross-strand billing continues, and the strand boundaries keep softening · **Grounded**

Every multi-act bill in the catalogue mixes BMEM strands — cross-strand rate
1.00. Genre tags in the artist records are increasingly plural for
later-forming acts. Folk motifs are documented in 20 of 63 acts across every
strand, not just the four folk-strand acts.

*Expect:* genre labels to keep losing descriptive power, and fusion pairings
(folk × hip-hop, indigenous × rock) to keep arriving through platforms and
festival programming rather than through scenes.
*Falsified if:* new events show single-strand bills as the norm.

## 4. Institutional and brand funding grows relative to label funding · **Indicated**

The catalogue's most consequential recent events are funded by non-commercial
motives: a state-adjacent youth platform (Joy Bangla Concert — free entry,
32,000 registrations in 2018) and a beverage brand's cultural-prestige project
(Coke Studio Bangla, 2022, with the folk strand's leading practitioner as
producer). Warfaze's Ekushey Padak (2026) is state recognition arriving where
commercial validation once was.

*Expect:* more brand-funded platforms and state-linked commemorative
programming as primary visibility routes for non-pop strands.
*Falsified if:* label-driven releases or ticketed commercial festivals become
the dominant documented route again.
*Political caveat:* the Joy Bangla Concert was tied to a specific political
formation. Its funding model may not survive a change of government, and
nothing in this dataset can predict that. This is a forecast about *funding
type*, not about that series continuing.

## 5. Hip-hop's documented presence grows sharply — from a base the record
understates · **Indicated**

The strand's catalogued lineage runs 1993 → 2024 with formations still
occurring in the last year of the catalogue, the only strand of which that is
true. Its live and network presence in this dataset is near zero, which the
hip-hop case study establishes as a sourcing failure rather than a fact about
the music.

*Expect:* the apparent growth of Bangladeshi hip-hop over the next few years to
be substantially a measurement effect — the scene becoming legible to the
sources researchers use, rather than newly large.
*Falsified if:* systematic collection of hip-hop event data still finds a thin
live circuit.

## 6. Long-lived bands persist through personnel turnover · **Grounded**

Still-active shares are 92% or higher in every strand. Acts formed in 1972,
1976, 1984 and 1991 remain on 2020s bills. The mechanism is documented lineup
churn: Vikings' founding vocalist leaving after 23 years, Artcell's lineup
changes, Warfaze's multi-generational turnover.

*Expect:* the classic-band circuit to persist into the 2030s as an institution
staffed by successive musicians rather than as a fixed set of people.
*Falsified if:* catalogued acts begin disbanding rather than replacing members.
*Caveat:* survivorship bias — the catalogue was built from acts notable enough
to document, which selects for survival.

## 7. Heavy metal stops recruiting; alternative and hip-hop keep forming · **Speculative**

Heavy metal's latest catalogued formation is 2007. Alternative/indie continues
to 2018 and hip-hop to 2024.

This may be real generational closure, or it may be that the catalogue was
built outward from a well-documented metal core and newer metal bands simply
have not been researched. **This project cannot separate those two, and the
forecast should not be relied on until it can.** Testing it requires
deliberately searching for post-2007 metal formations; if few exist, the trend
is real.

## 8. What this project cannot forecast

Stated explicitly, because these are exactly the questions a music-industry
forecast is usually expected to answer:

- **Streaming economics** — royalties, per-stream revenue, playlist economics.
  No API access; no data.
- **Audience size or composition** — no listener-side data at any point in this
  project. Every audience claim here is inferred from co-billing and from what
  artists and journalists said.
- **AI in production and recommendation** — the proposal anticipates this and
  the catalogue contains no evidence about it. Speculating would be inventing.
- **Commercial viability of any strand** — no sales, ticketing or revenue data.

The honest summary: this project can forecast **form, venue and institutional
structure** with some confidence, and can forecast **money and audience** not at
all. Closing that second gap needs the streaming and ticketing access listed in
`docs/roadmap.md`.
