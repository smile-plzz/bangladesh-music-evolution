# Literature Review (Working Draft)

This document collects and organizes scholarly and secondary sources relevant to the evolution of Bangladeshi music culture, with emphasis on band/rock/metal scenes, global influence and local adaptation, audience formation, and digital transformation.

---

## 1. Academic & Scholarly Sources

### Directly on Bangladeshi / Bangla Rock & Metal

- **Quader, Shams Bin & Redden, Guy (2014).** "Approaching the Underground: The production of alternatives in the Bangladeshi metal scene." *Cultural Studies*.  
  Ethnographic study of the underground urban metal scene in Bangladesh. Argues that youth frustration with national conditions is channelled into building alternative cultural space. Key for understanding subcultural identity, cover-band origins, and the shift to original Bangla metal compositions. Based on interviews (2012) with scene participants.

- **Hasan, Mubashar (2015).** "Rock ‘n’ Roll, social change and democratisation in Bangladesh."  
  Positions Bangladeshi rock as a cultural force supporting liberal values, religious tolerance, and critique of authoritarianism/extremism. Useful for the socio-political dimension of the research.

- **Mitra, Udita (2014).** *Exploring youth: A sociological study of Bangla bands* (PhD thesis, Jadavpur University).  
  Sociological examination of Bangla bands and youth culture. Valuable comparative/parallel material from the broader Bengali cultural sphere.

- **Mukherjee, Kamalini.** "Bangla Rock: exploring the counterculture and dissidence in post-colonial Bengali popular music."  
  Focuses more on West Bengal but provides theoretical framing for vernacular rock as counterculture and oral history in post-colonial Bengal.

- **Yoon, Jisun (2019).** "Cultural Hybridity & Identity of Bangladesh reflected in Popular music: Focusing on the Fusion band BANGLA." *Journal of South Asian Studies*.  
  Examines cultural hybridity through the fusion band Bangla and Baul/Lalon influences. Relevant for the Folk & Folk Fusion strand and local adaptation processes.

- **Autul et al. (2024).** "Rocking Across Borders: An Analysis of the Musical Differences between Bangladesh and West-Bengal Rock Songs Using Spotify Audio Features."  
  Computational comparison using Spotify audio features. Methodologically useful for the quantitative/streaming dimension of the present study.

### Broader Theoretical Lenses

- Cultural Evolution Theory
- Uses and Gratifications Theory (media/audience studies)
- Diffusion of Innovations (Rogers) — useful for technology adoption (cassette → streaming)
- Bourdieu’s Theory of Cultural Capital — taste, distinction, and subcultural capital in music scenes
- Social Identity Theory — fan communities and genre-based identity
- Popular music studies literature on globalization, localization, and hybridity (e.g., work on non-Western rock/metal scenes)
- Computational social science / digital humanities approaches to music preference networks and recommendation systems

## 4. Preliminary Synthesis Notes

**Gaps identified** (justifying the present study):
- Limited systematic, multi-genre, data-driven mapping of the full contemporary ecosystem.
- Few studies that explicitly link *sonic evolution* (production, composition, instrumentation) to *listener networks* and *concert culture*.
- Scarce computational work using public digital footprints at scale for Bangladesh.
- Need for a coherent framework (BMEM) that integrates global influence → local adaptation → audience formation → industry change.

**Emerging patterns from existing literature**:
1. Early bands (Souls, Miles, Feedback, Feelings/Nagar Baul) established commercial band culture and Bangla-language rock/pop-rock in the late 1970s–1980s.
2. Mid-to-late 1980s: Warfaze, Rockstrata, In Dhaka, Aces introduce heavy metal via covers of Iron Maiden, Metallica, Deep Purple, etc., then shift to original Bangla material.
3. Late 1990s–2000s: Artcell and others crystallize progressive metal with explicit Dream Theater / Metallica / Pink Floyd / Opeth influences while writing complex Bangla lyrics and drawing on local literary and folk sensibilities.
4. Parallel streams: indie/alternative (Meghdol et al.), pop commercial success, hip-hop growth, folk-fusion revival (Arnob, Coke Studio Bangla).
5. Digital platforms (YouTube → Spotify) and social media have reconfigured discovery, monetization, and community formation since the mid-2000s.
