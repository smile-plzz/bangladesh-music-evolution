# Core Genres Under Study

This document outlines the primary genres examined in the research, with representative Bangladeshi artists and the major global influences hypothesized for investigation (not assumed as proven).

---

## 1. Mainstream Rock

**Representative Artists**  
James, Nagar Baul, Miles, LRB (Love Runs Blind), Souls, Feedback, Ark, Renaissance

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- Classic Rock & Blues Rock: Led Zeppelin, Deep Purple, Pink Floyd, Dire Straits, Eric Clapton
- Hard Rock traditions

**Key Research Angles**  
- Multi-generational appeal and nostalgia function
- Large-scale concert culture (open-air, stadium, national festivals)
- Role in national cultural memory
- Evolution of songwriting and production from cassette/CD eras to streaming

---

## 2. Progressive Rock / Progressive Metal

**Representative Artists**  
Artcell, Karnival, Owned

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- Dream Theater, Tool, Opeth, Pink Floyd, Porcupine Tree

**Key Research Angles**  
- Incorporation of Bangla literature and philosophy into progressive forms
- Technical and compositional evolution across albums
- Distinction between Bangladeshi progressive music and its global counterparts
- Dedicated fan communities and festival culture
- Atmospheric and conceptual storytelling development (especially Artcell)

---

## 3. Heavy Metal

**Representative Artists**  
Warfaze, Powersurge, Mechanix, Cryptic Fate

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- Iron Maiden, Metallica, Megadeth, Judas Priest, Black Sabbath

**Key Research Angles**  
- Technical evolution (guitar styles, production quality)
- Adaptation of heavy metal aesthetics to Bangla lyrics and local themes
- Live performance intensity and festival communities
- Long-term fan loyalty and subcultural identity
- Production quality trajectory from early recordings to modern releases

---

## 4. Alternative / Indie

**Representative Artists**  
Meghdol, Ashes, Shohojia, Conclusion

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- Radiohead, Arctic Monkeys, Fleet Foxes, Bon Iver, The National
- Grunge-era emotional lyricism (Nirvana, Pearl Jam) as a possible earlier vector

**Key Research Angles**  
- Independent production and DIY ethos
- Experimental songwriting and literary/poetic influences
- Smaller venue / intimate gig culture
- Community-driven audiences and artistic identity
- Sonic trajectory: psychedelic/alternative foundations toward richer textures and experimentation

## 5. Pop

**Representative Artists**  
Habib Wahid, Tahsan, Minar, Imran, Nancy, Kona

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- Contemporary global pop, R&B, electronic pop
- K-pop production trends and aesthetics

- Commercial production and mainstream audience behavior

## 6. Hip-Hop / Rap

**Representative Artists**  
Stoic Bliss, Muza, Hannan, Jalali Set

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- American Hip-Hop traditions
- UK Drill and related UK scenes
- South Asian (especially Indian and diasporic) hip-hop

**Key Research Angles**  
- Urban identity and language evolution (Bangla slang, code-switching)
- Digital-first distribution and promotion strategies
- Social commentary and political themes

---

## 7. Folk & Folk Fusion

**Representative Artists / Projects**  
Arnob, Coke Studio Bangla collaborators, traditional Baul artists, various folk-fusion bands and projects

**Global Influence Vectors to Investigate**  
- World Music movements
- Acoustic and contemporary folk revival (e.g., Bon Iver, Fleet Foxes, Mumford & Sons as possible reference points)
- Cross-genre fusion practices internationally

**Key Research Angles**  
- Cultural preservation vs. modern reinterpretation
- Fusion with rock, jazz, electronic, and other contemporary forms
- Role of platforms such as Coke Studio Bangla in visibility and hybridity
- Continuity of Baul and other traditional streams into contemporary scenes

---

## Cross-Genre Themes

Across all genres the research examines:

1. **Sound Evolution** — instrumentation, production, composition, vocal style over time
2. **Local Adaptation** — how global materials are transformed through language, literature, folk elements, and identity
3. **Listener Communities** — preference networks and identity formation
4. **Concert Culture** — how live experience differs by genre and audience
5. **Industry Position** — relationship to labels, independence, digital platforms, monetization

These themes feed directly into the Bangladesh Musical Ecosystem Model (BMEM).

## Platform Note: Coke Studio Bangla

Coke Studio Bangla launched on 7 February 2022 as the Bangladeshi edition of the international Coke Studio franchise (following Pakistan, 2008, and India, 2011), positioning fusion music — blending classical, folk, Sufi, qawwali, and contemporary genres — as its signature format. Shayan Chowdhury Arnob served as music producer/director for the first season, directly tying the platform to the Folk & Folk Fusion genre's leading practitioner. Season 1 featured regional folk traditions (Bhawaiya from northern Bengal, Maizbhandari from Chattogram, Bhatiyali riverine folk music) reinterpreted with contemporary bands such as Shironamhin and Jalali Set, and drew on the legacies of Rabindranath Tagore and Baul song traditions. As a corporate-backed, high-production platform, Coke Studio Bangla functions as a significant industry mechanism for cross-genre visibility and hybridity within the BMEM's Industry Transformation dimension.
